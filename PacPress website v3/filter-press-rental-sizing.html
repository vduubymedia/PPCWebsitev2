How to Size a Rental Filter Press

Sizing a rental filter press comes down to a handful of factors: how much material you need to process, how concentrated it is, and how dry the discharged solids need to be. This guide explains what matters and what to have ready before you call.

Direct Answer

PacPress sizes rental filter presses using cubic foot filtration capacity, expected cycle time, solids percentage, daily volume, and material characteristics. Sharing these details with a specialist is the fastest way to confirm an appropriate configuration and current availability.

Why Cubic Foot Capacity Matters

Cubic foot capacity is the standard way PacPress rates filter press size. It describes how much filter cake volume the press can hold in a single cycle — a bigger number means more solids processed per cycle, which matters most when daily volume is high.

A press that is too small for your volume will need more cycles per day to keep up, which can strain labor and timeline. A press that is too large adds unnecessary rental cost. Matching capacity to your actual volume is the goal.

What Cycle Time Means

Cycle time is the time it takes to fill the press, build a full cake, and discharge solids — start to finish, one complete cycle. It depends on the material being processed, feed pressure, solids loading, and filter cloth condition.

Cycle time directly determines how many cycles you can realistically run per day, which in turn affects whether a given press size can keep up with your daily volume.

Why Solids Percentage Matters

Solids percentage describes how concentrated your slurry or sludge is. Higher solids content usually means faster cake buildup and can change how the feed pump and press should be configured. Lower solids content may mean a longer fill phase before cake starts to form.

If you don’t know your exact solids percentage, a general description of the material (thin slurry, thick sludge, etc.) is still useful starting information.

What Daily Volume Means

Daily volume is how much material you expect to process each day, on average. Combined with cycle time and press capacity, it determines whether a rental size can keep up with your schedule, or whether you may need a larger press or a longer operating window.

Why Material and Slurry Characteristics Matter

Different materials behave differently under pressure. Cement slurry, mining fines, biosolids, and process wastewater all filter at different rates and can require different filter cloth, feed pressure, or pretreatment. Describing your material as specifically as possible helps PacPress recommend the right configuration.

What Support Equipment May Be Needed

Beyond the press itself, a project may require a feed pump, hoses and connections, a power source, and space for setup and cake discharge. PacPress reviews these requirements and identifies the selected support equipment in the project quote.

Before You Call

Sizing Checklist

Having these details ready speeds up your quote — but don’t worry if you don’t have all of them yet.

  • Material or slurry type — what is being dewatered or clarified.
  • Daily or total volume — how much material needs processing, and over what timeframe.
  • Solids percentage — if known, even an estimate helps.
  • Desired cake dryness — how dry the discharged solids need to be.
  • Project timeline — expected start date and rental duration.
  • Site location — for freight planning.
  • Available utilities — power and water access on site.
  • Support equipment needs — whether you already have pumps, tanks, or hoses.
